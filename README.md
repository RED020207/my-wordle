How it works? It's a basic word-guessing game, where the program itself encrypts a random word in a specific way and its the user's job to try guess what word it actually is!

Now it's time to talk about the specifics of this game's design. Firstly, each level has a name. Level 1 is called 'Super Easy', Level 2 is called 'Easy', Level 3 is called 'Moderate', Level 4 is called 'Hard', Level 5 is called 'Super Hard' and Level 6 is called 'Impossible'. Player can ask for a hint by simply entering 'H' and can quit the game at any point by entering 'Q'. Single letters aren't allowed as valid word choices for word guesses and must display an appropriate error message in ALL CAPS.
First, we clarify the hint system. As mentioned earlier, five hints are allowed on each level. The first hint is given by default after the encrypted word is provided and always vaguely reveals 'how' (or using what 'logic', if you prefer that) the word was encrypted, usually with eloquent, funny or clever wordplay. The second and third hint reveal the random 'factor' that might've been used along with the 'how' (or 'logic') to encrypt that word. This 'factor' will ALWAYS be a random number between 1 and 9 (both inclusive), while playing a major role in how the encrypted word will turn out. Specifically, the second hint will reveal if the 'factor' is an even number or an odd number, essentially halving the number of possibilities. The third hint will reveal if the 'factor' is a prime number or not, further narrowing down the possibilities significantly. The fourth hint will reveal a significant chunk of the 'logic' used for encryption, still a little vague but in noticeably clearer and simpler language. Lastly, the final or fifth hint will reveal the answer and exactly what the logic of encryption was (not specifically in this particular case, but the general logic followed for every word encrypted at this level), in very clear and precise language. Keep in mind, 'H' can only be entered when the player is already in the middle of guessing an encrypted word on a particular level. Entering 'H' before choosing a level to play on should display a funny message saying that the game hasn't even started yet, and after one guessing game is over (either by the player correctly guessing the encrypted word or by the game ending with the fifth hint) the list of available levels to play will be shown again for the player to choose once more for the next round.
Second, we must bring up the quitting mechanic. If the player hasn't chosen a level to play on yet and enters 'Q', the game will display a generic thank you message for playing and simply end the program. If, however, the user is already in the middle of guessing on a certain level, entering 'Q' will first display the right answer of the ongoing guessing round along with the explanation of the encryption (like the fifth hint would) and only then display the generic thank you message before ending the program.
Third, we discuss the increasingly complex mechanics of each level:
Level 1 - Encrypts the randomly chosen word by simply reversing its letters. So 'chair' becomes 'riahc' and 'heart' becomes 'traeh'. Because of how simple this logic of encryption is, the first hint reveals what its supposed to about this reversion logic, but the second, third and fourth hint refuse to reveal anything else and instead just funnily taunts the player (like, "Really, you can't even figure this one out?" or "Are you for real??" or "Oh my god, you can't actually be this stupid!"). The fifth hint obviously reveals the correct decrypted word and also the explanation for the encryption, but with an emphasis on how simple it was (like, "It was just reversed." or "All I did was flip the word."). The 'factor' chosen for this level will always be 1 and contributes in no way to the encryption.
Level 2 - Encrypts the randomly chosen word by shifting the letters forward or backward by the randomly chosen 'factor'. An odd 'factor' makes the letters shift forward, and an even 'factor' makes them shift backward. Also, the letters of the english alphabet are ordered from 1 to 26 for all relevant purposes throughout this program (this doesn't change their ASCII values), in a circular fashion. So if the 'factor' randomly chosen is 4 and the word randomly chosen for encryption is 'after', each letter will go 4 steps backward in a circular fashion to form the encrypted word 'wbpan'.
Level 3 - Encrypts the randomly chosen word by shifting the letters forward or backward by the randomly chosen 'factor' like the last level, but alternatively this time! So, an odd 'factor' will make the first letter go forward by this 'factor', the second letter go backward by this 'factor', the third letter go forward by this 'factor' and so on. An even 'factor', on the other hand, makes the first letter go backward by this 'factor', the second one go forward by this 'factor', the third one go backward by this 'factor' and so on. So if the 'factor' randomly chosen is 3 and the word randomly chosen for encryption is 'now', then the first letter will go forward by 3, the second letter will go backward by 3 and so on, in a circular fashion of course, to form the encrypted word 'qlz'.
Level 4 - Encrypts the randomly chosen word by shuffling adjacent letters a particular number of times determined by the randomly chosen 'factor', only up to the penultimate letter (because the last letter doesn't have a next letter to shuffle with). Since shuflling adjacent letters an even number of times will essentially keep their relative positions unchanged, the 'factor' chosen in this level will always be odd. This won't, however, change how the second hint (which reveals the parity of the factor) behaves. So if the 'factor' chosen randomly is 9 and the word chosen randomly for encryption is 'love', then it becomes 'ovel'. For the purpose of coding this logic, just interchanging each letter with its adjacent one (up to the penultimate letter, of course) is enough to produce the same effect (yeah, for simplicity and efficiency's sake, you don't gotta actually shuffle them multiple times)!
Level 5 - Encrypts the randomly chosen word by converting multiplying each letter by it's corresponding position number in the alphabet series with the randomly chosen factor raised to a power corresponding to the letter's position in the word, then converts the result into a corresponding letter of the english alphabet! Too complicated? Let's take an example. Say, the letter in question is 'c' and it's the fourth letter in the word, then the result for it will be 3 * (f^4) because c is the third letter of the english alphabet, 'f' being the randomly chosen 'factor'. It's basically a quadratic equation where the letters themselves are the coefficients and the the 'factor' is the variable! So 'ace' could be written as 1f + 3(f^2) + 5*(f^3) where 'f' is again the randomly chosen factor and each term is the result for each corresponding letter. How to convert the numeric result for each letter into an english alphabet? Simple! If, say, 78 is the corresponding numeric term for a letter, just keep on subtracting 26 from it until it gives a number equal to or less than 26. So, 78 subracted by 26 twice gives us 26, that's the number of 'z' in the english alphabet so the converted alphabet is 'z'! Similarly, 53 would be 1, so it's 'a'! Basically, result%25=letter, with 0 corresponding to 'z', 1 corresponding to 'a' and so on. If 73 81 53 were the resultant numeric terms after the quadratic conversion for each letter, the corresponding encrypted word would be 11 3 1 or simply 'kca'!
Level 6 - Remember the quadratic equation made from the letters of the randomly chosen word for the last level? Same logic here, except we also differentiate that equation multiple times, as given by the 'factor'! So, if a word can be converted into 1f + 3(f^2) + 5*(f^3) and the factor is, say, 4, we differentiate this equation four times. The resultant equation is.........0 + 0 + 0? Oops! Guess we differentiated too many times, so every term vanished to 0. To make sure this doesn't happen, the 'factor' chosen for this level must be capped at a number equal to the length of the word itself! So for the above word 'ace' and it's corresponding quadratic equation 1f + 3(f^2) + 5*(f^3) the maximum allowed 'factor' should be 3. Say, the factor is 2! So we differentiate this equation twice, giving us 0 + 6 + 30f. If the 'factor' were 2, that would become 0 + 6 + 60 and going by the same rules as level 5, that would translate to the encrypted word 'zfh'. And just like that, we converted 'ace' to 'zfh'!

Next, let me just briefly summarize all the things I did and what this project is about:
1. It's a word-based game called Wordle (sorry if it matches with any other game's name out there), where the program encrypts a random word for you. You guess how it was encrypted and decrypt it accordingly to guess the correct original word.
2. There's no score system here. But there's a system of hints, each level letting you have five hints, of which the first hint is given by default and the fifth hint just reveals the answer (along with an explanation of the encryption). You can also quit at any point in the game.
3. There are 6 levels to this, each with increasing difficulty. The difficulty curve is lowkey brutal, my bad!
4. I don't like names for my variables and functions that are too long or have underscores, so I specifically prompted Gemini to follow this personal preference of mine while coding this.
5. If you are wondering how or why the hints are kinda differently worded sometimes, I kinda used a randomizing mechanic and there's a set of 4 differently worded texts to choose from for each 'unique' kinda hint when displaying it. The randomiation uses rand() and seeding with srand() once at the beginning of the program for better randomization each time the game is started anew.
6. There's also a 'factor' system. Basically, if the logic of encrypting a word is shifting each letter by a particular number (meaning, shifting 'A' by 2 would make it 'C'), then this number is the 'factor' and determines what the encrypted word will be. The 'factor' will always be between 1 and 9 (both inclusive) and shall be selected randomly for each round.
7. DO NOT READ BELOW POINTS IF YOU DON'T WANNA GET SPOILED ABOUT THE LEVELS!
8. Level 1 - The word is simply reversed. If you ask for hints for this one, the game will taunt you because its too easy.
9. Level 2 - Each letter of the word is shifted forward or backward by a number. Odd numbers make the letters move forward and even numbers move the letters backward. The shifting is circular, so 'A' shifted backwards by 2 makes 'Y'.
10. Level 3 - Same logic as before, but the shifting alternates. If the number of shift is odd, first letter is shifted forward by this number, second one is moved backward by this number, and so on. Opposite applies for even number of shift.
11. Level 4 - Adjacent letters are shuffled a particular number of times. Basically, each letter of the word is interchanged with the next one, from left to right (up to the second-last letter, because the last letter doesn't have a next letter after it to be shuffled with).
12. Level 5 - The letters are the coefficients, the randomly chosen number is the variable and the length of the word is the degree of a polynomial. So 'and' becomes f + 14f^2 + 4f^3 where f is the randomly chosen number (as mentioned earlier, its always between 1 and 9, and the second & third hints always hint at this number). The resultant term of each term determines what that letter will be encrypted into, so if the second letter results in 56 then 'n' here becomes 'd' (56 - 26*2 = 4, which means the 4th letter in the english alphabet system).
13. Level 6 - Same logic as before, but the polynomial formed is differentiated a particular number of times (same number of times as the randomly chosen number). Don't worry, I know differentiating too many times will reduce all terms to 0, but that's avoided by limiting the number of times differentiated (and the number randomly chosen for this level) to the maximum length of the word. There's too much guessing involved here, as you can tell if you think a little harder about this, so it's truly kinda impossible to do this level.
